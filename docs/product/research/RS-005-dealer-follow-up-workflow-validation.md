# RS-005: Dealer Follow-Up Workflow Validation Package

- **Research status:** RESEARCH REQUIRED
- **Lifecycle:** Captured
- **Prepared:** 2026-08-24
- **Related decision:** [PDR-007](../decisions/PDR-007-bounded-internal-follow-up-disposition.md)
- **Related features:** SP-F016, SP-F016-A, SP-F017
- **Research owner:** Product

## Objective and Boundary

Validate how dealership staff handle declined or deferred service work after identification and whether the bounded `SP-F016-A` concept fits that work. This package prepares research; it contains no dealer findings and does not approve implementation or release scope.

The study must establish evidence about current behavior, pain, workarounds, desired outcomes, and concept fit before PDR-007 is revised or `SP-F016-A` changes status. Phone or email availability is never evidence of consent. Do not test SMS, WhatsApp, email sending, automated outreach, campaign management, or marketing automation as part of this concept.

## Participant Profile

Recruit people who perform or oversee the workflow, not only commercial sponsors:

| Participant | Relevant perspective | Context to record without personal data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service Advisor | Daily identification, customer interaction, and outcome recording | Dealer type, service volume band if volunteered, current tools, market |
| Service Manager | Ownership, overdue handling, quality control, and visibility | Team structure, number of sites if relevant, management cadence |
| Aftersales Manager | Cross-workflow accountability and commercial measurement | Scope of responsibility, reporting practices, systems involved |
| Dealer Principal / GM | Business value, risk, pilot approval, and investment questions | Dealer-group context, decision responsibilities, evidence expectations |

Seek both operating and management perspectives for each participating dealer where practical. Record actual coverage and gaps; do not imply representativeness from an unrecorded or convenience sample. Do not collect participant names, customer records, credentials, or customer contact details in this research package.

## Session Plan: 30-45 Minutes

| Time | Activity | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 min | Opening and permission | Set boundaries and confirm note-taking permission |
| 3–15 min | Current workflow walkthrough | Observe current behavior before exposing the concept |
| 15–22 min | Pain, workarounds, information, and measurement | Understand consequences and existing adaptations |
| 22–33 min | PDR-007 concept validation | Test ownership, lifecycle, dispositions, and safety rules |
| 33–40 min | Prioritization, demo, commercial, and pilot questions | Identify what matters without making claims |
| 40–45 min | Closing and correction | Capture missing context and let the participant correct the summary |

If only 30 minutes are available, preserve current-workflow discovery, safety, concept fit, and closing correction. Shorten prioritization and commercial probes first.

## Opening Script

“Thank you for speaking with us. We are researching what happens after declined or deferred service work is identified. We want to understand your current process before discussing a possible AutoVision workflow. There are no right answers, and the concept may be wrong or incomplete. We are not evaluating you or your team.

Please do not share customer names, contact details, payment information, credentials, or other sensitive records. We would like to take notes about the workflow and may record exact wording only with your permission. We will separate what you do today from reactions to the proposed concept. May we proceed and take notes?”

Record permission as `Granted`, `Declined`, or `Restricted`. Do not record audio unless a separately approved research process governs recording, storage, access, and deletion.

## Evidence Labels

Tag each note so observations do not collapse into conclusions:

- **CURRENT BEHAVIOR:** what happens today;
- **PAIN:** an adverse consequence described by the participant;
- **WORKAROUND:** an adaptation to current tools or process;
- **DESIRED OUTCOME:** what better work would achieve, without assuming a feature;
- **CONCEPT FEEDBACK:** reaction after the PDR-007 concept is introduced;
- **EVIDENCE:** an observed artifact or participant statement, marked as exact quote or paraphrase.

Never convert a concept reaction into current-behavior evidence. Record the interviewer’s interpretation separately from participant evidence.

## Current Workflow Discovery

Start with a recent representative example. Ask for a walkthrough, not agreement with a proposed process.

1. **CURRENT BEHAVIOR:** “Think about the last time declined or deferred work was identified. What happened next?”
   - Who first noticed it, and in which system or artifact?
   - What event moved it from identification to action, if anything?
   - Where did the process stop or leave the normal workflow?
2. **CURRENT BEHAVIOR:** “How does someone become responsible for the next step?”
   - Is responsibility explicit or understood informally?
   - Who can establish or change it?
   - What happens across shifts, absence, branches, or teams?
3. **CURRENT BEHAVIOR:** “How is follow-up planned?”
   - Is there a date, time, priority, reminder, list, calendar, or manual memory aid?
   - Who decides when follow-up should happen?
4. **CURRENT BEHAVIOR:** “What happens when an item becomes overdue?”
   - Who notices and who acts?
   - Are reminders or escalations used? What triggers them?
5. **CURRENT BEHAVIOR:** “How does management know what is open, completed, or unresolved?”
   - Which views, meetings, exports, or reports are used?
   - What is difficult to verify?
6. **PAIN:** “Where does this process most often slow down, get lost, or create risk?”
   - Ask for consequence, reported frequency, and severity in the participant’s own terms.
7. **WORKAROUND:** “What have you or your team created to make this work today?”
   - What is useful about that workaround?
   - What would be costly or risky to replace?

## Accountability Model

Do not present self-claim as the default. Ask these questions before showing PDR-007:

1. “Who should decide that a person is accountable for an item, and why?”
2. “When, if ever, would someone choose an item for themselves?”
3. “When is manager assignment necessary?”
4. “When must ownership move to another person, team, or branch?”
5. “What should happen when the owner is absent or leaves the role?”
6. “Does the work need a due date, a due time, both, or neither?”
7. “What makes a reminder helpful rather than noise?”
8. “Which overdue conditions require escalation, to whom, and with what information?”
9. “Would a personal queue, team queue, branch queue, or another view match current work? What makes that view useful?”

Capture evidence separately for self-claim, manager assignment, reassignment, team/branch queues, due date/time, reminders, and escalation. “Required,” “useful,” and “unnecessary” are distinct findings.

## Disposition Vocabulary

First ask, “What words does your team use to describe how one of these items ends?” Record the participant vocabulary before showing the candidate terms.

Then test each PDR-007 disposition in randomized or context-appropriate order:

| Candidate term | Neutral probe |
| --- | --- |
| `FOLLOW_UP_REQUIRED` | “What would this mean in your process? What remains unresolved?” |
| `NO_FOLLOW_UP_DUPLICATE` | “How do you identify and handle a duplicate today?” |
| `NO_FOLLOW_UP_ALREADY_COMPLETED` | “How is already-completed work recognized and recorded?” |
| `NO_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_APPLICABLE` | “When is an item validly not applicable, and who decides?” |
| `DATA_REVIEW_REQUIRED` | “What happens when the source or identity information appears wrong?” |
| `CUSTOMER_INTEREST_RECORDED` | “What evidence would justify recording interest, and what would this not prove?” |
| `CUSTOMER_DECLINED_RECORDED` | “Which kinds of decline matter operationally? Do reasons or timing matter?” |
| `NO_RESPONSE_RECORDED` | “What constitutes an attempt and no response in your current process?” |

For each term ask:

- What phrase would staff actually use?
- Is it an outcome, an intermediate state, a reason, or unnecessary?
- Does another term already cover it?
- What outcomes are missing?
- What evidence or role is needed to record it?
- Could it be misused or interpreted as proof of customer contact or conversion?

After reviewing terms, show lifecycle separately: `UNCLAIMED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `FOLLOW_UP_PLANNED`, and `DISPOSITION_RECORDED`. Ask participants to sort example cases into lifecycle versus outcome and explain ambiguities. Do not teach the distinction until their initial interpretation is captured.

## Actionability and Safety

Use one realistic example per state. Ask what users should see, be allowed to do, and be blocked from doing.

### READY_TO_ACTION

- “What checks, if any, should happen before accountability or a next step is recorded?”
- “Which actions are safe, and which still require another decision?”

### REVIEW_REQUIRED

- “Who performs the review, using what information?”
- “What establishes that review is complete?”
- “Before confirmation, should users claim, schedule, disposition, or record an externally obtained outcome? Why?”
- “What should happen if reviewers disagree?”

### SUPPRESSED

- “What should remain visible, and what must be blocked?”
- “Can suppression ever be corrected or reversed? Who decides and what evidence is required?”
- “How should duplicate or already-completed work differ from other suppression reasons?”

Ask across all states: “What is the most harmful action the product could accidentally permit?” Capture safety disagreements as unresolved findings, not majority preference.

## Information Needs

Begin with a blank-state exercise: “Before deciding what to do, what information do you need?” Only then show the categories available in the current detail experience.

Sort each item with the participant:

| Classification | Test |
| --- | --- |
| Essential | A safe or useful decision cannot be made without it |
| Useful secondary | Helps explanation or confidence but can be progressively disclosed |
| Unnecessary | Does not affect this decision or adds avoidable cognitive/privacy cost |

Probe customer/vehicle/service context, opportunity type, potential value and currency, evidence, explanation, source/provenance, actionability, suppression/review reason, previous activity, owner, due state, and disposition history. Ask what information should never appear in the primary view. Preserve decision-critical information first and progressively disclose audit history, provenance, and notes.

## Measurement Discovery

Do not propose targets. Ask how effectiveness is judged today and what decision each measure supports:

1. Which counts or outcomes are reviewed today?
2. How are follow-ups due, overdue, and completed defined?
3. What establishes that an appointment was created because of the follow-up?
4. What establishes that a job converted, and over what period?
5. How is recovered revenue attributed without confusing potential with realized value?
6. Is gross profit available and trustworthy? If not, what is used instead?
7. How is aging understood and segmented?
8. Is advisor or owner performance measured? What fairness or gaming concerns arise?
9. Which measures would cause a manager to change an operational decision?
10. What other dealer-defined measure is more useful than the list above?

For every requested measure capture definition, source system, responsible owner, availability, latency, known gaps, and risk of misleading attribution. Requested metrics are research evidence, not approved KPIs.

## Data, Privacy, and Operations

1. “When are notes necessary, and what should never be written in them?”
2. “Who needs to read notes or history?”
3. “How long is the information operationally useful, and what retention rules apply?”
4. “Which contact fields are needed to make an internal decision?”
5. “Where is consent or preferred-channel authority recorded today?” Do not infer it from phone/email presence.
6. “Which actions must be auditable, and who reviews the audit?”
7. “When should a disposition be corrected or reopened? What reason or approval is required?”
8. “How are duplicates recognized, linked, and resolved?”
9. “How is already-completed work confirmed?”
10. “What happens when source data is wrong? Which system owns correction?”
11. “What information creates privacy, employee-monitoring, or customer-trust concern?”

Do not collect example customer contact data or note content. Describe fields and rules abstractly.

## Proposed Concept Validation

Only after current-state discovery, introduce this neutral summary:

“AutoVision is considering a bounded internal workflow linked to an opportunity. The current concept would allow an authenticated user to claim an item for themselves, record a due date and short internal note when needed, progress a controlled lifecycle, and record a controlled disposition with auditable history. It would not send messages, infer consent, manage campaigns, correct source master data, or prove recovered revenue. The concept is not approved for release.”

Ask:

1. “What part, if any, fits your current work?”
2. “What would fail or create extra work?”
3. “Which responsibility is missing or incorrectly assigned?”
4. “What would you remove from the first version?”
5. “What must change before this could be piloted safely?”
6. “What existing tool or process would this need to coexist with?”
7. “What would make you decide not to use it?”

## Prioritization

Use forced trade-offs rather than asking whether every capability is valuable:

1. “If only two improvements were possible first, which would reduce the most operational risk or effort?”
2. “Which is more important first: clear ownership, due-state visibility, controlled outcome, or management overview? Why?”
3. “Which capability can remain manual during a pilot?”
4. “Which missing capability makes the concept unusable rather than merely incomplete?”
5. “What should be visible immediately, and what belongs behind progressive disclosure?”

Record ranking rationale and role context, not only the chosen item.

## Demo and Commercial Discovery

These questions improve product validation and communication, but answers do not become sales claims without implementation and evidence:

1. “During a demonstration, what would you need to see to understand where an opportunity came from?”
2. “Which safety example would build confidence: duplicate, already completed, ambiguous evidence, or another case?”
3. “Which part of the future journey would need to be visible rather than explained by a presenter?”
4. “What objection or misunderstanding would you expect from a manager, advisor, principal, or IT stakeholder?”
5. “What proof would you expect in a PPT or short demo video before agreeing to a pilot conversation?”
6. “Which claims would make you skeptical?”
7. “What operational question must a pilot answer before a purchase decision?”
8. “Who would approve a pilot, and which evidence would they review?”

Store product findings under RS-005. Feed evidence-backed question wording into the [demo business question register](../demo/demo-business-question-register.md). Commercial owners may use themes for preparation, but must not turn participant expectations into product capability, pricing, market, or ROI claims.

## Closing Questions

1. “What important part of the workflow did we not ask about?”
2. “Who has a different perspective on this process?”
3. “What is the biggest risk in changing the current process?”
4. “What would a safe, useful pilot need to protect?”
5. “Here is our brief understanding of what you said. What is wrong or missing?”
6. “May we contact the research coordinator through the existing recruitment process if clarification is needed?”

Do not capture personal contact details in the evidence record.

## Cross-Session Synthesis

Do not aggregate until evidence records retain role and dealer context. Synthesis must report:

- participant and context coverage, including gaps;
- repeated patterns and meaningful exceptions;
- competing workflows by role, dealer type, branch model, market, or system;
- exact versus paraphrased evidence counts;
- areas where terminology converges or remains fragmented;
- safety disagreements;
- requested measures and source feasibility;
- evidence that supports, changes, or rejects each PDR-007 assumption;
- negative cases and disconfirming evidence;
- confidence with rationale, never confidence by assertion.

## Decision Gate

Product applies the gate only after session evidence and cross-session synthesis are reviewed. Research alone does not change PDR, feature, release, or ADR status.

### GO

Recommend implementation planning without changing the bounded concept only when all are true:

- evidence covers daily operator and management perspectives, with recorded context gaps;
- a material accountability problem is evidenced in current behavior, not only concept preference;
- self-claim is workable for the first slice without requiring arbitrary assignment, reassignment, team, or branch authority;
- lifecycle/disposition separation and the controlled vocabulary are understood well enough to use safely;
- expected behavior for `READY_TO_ACTION`, `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, and `SUPPRESSED` matches PDR-007 safeguards;
- minimum information can be shown without unnecessary sensitive data;
- audit, correction, retention, and concurrency expectations fit the bounded architecture;
- no unresolved finding requires an excluded communication capability;
- remaining limitations can be stated and tested in a controlled pilot.

A GO recommendation still requires release approval. It does not change `SP-F016-A` from `DEFERRED` automatically.

### GO WITH CHANGES

Recommend revising the concept before implementation when the accountability problem is supported and safety is achievable, but evidence requires bounded changes such as terminology, lifecycle transitions, information hierarchy, due-state semantics, note handling, correction rules, or measurement events.

Required actions:

1. document each change and supporting evidence;
2. revise PDR-007 when product behavior or exclusions change;
3. revise the feature register if scope, persona, dependency, risk, or success measure changes;
4. update traceability and demo records;
5. rerun the gate against the revised concept before release approval.

### MORE RESEARCH REQUIRED

Continue research when any is true:

- operating and management perspectives are missing;
- findings conflict materially by role, dealer context, market, branch model, or system and the cause is unresolved;
- ownership or disposition terminology does not converge enough for a safe controlled model;
- privacy, retention, employee-monitoring, consent, or audit expectations are unresolved;
- participants cannot distinguish lifecycle from outcome after neutral explanation;
- measurement definitions or source ownership are too ambiguous for a pilot;
- concept feedback is positive but current-behavior evidence does not establish the problem.

RS-005 remains `Captured` or moves to `Researching` only when fieldwork actually begins. `SP-F016-A` remains `DEFERRED` and `Unassigned`.

### DO NOT PROCEED

Recommend not proceeding with `SP-F016-A` when evidence shows one or more of these:

- the accountability problem is not meaningful in the studied contexts or existing workflows solve it without material pain;
- the bounded workflow would increase unsafe customer action, duplicate work, or unmanageable staff surveillance risk;
- a useful first capability necessarily depends on excluded outreach, consent inference, campaign automation, or unsupported organization authority;
- required data cannot be minimized, tenant-contained, retained, or audited safely;
- no bounded ownership and disposition model can fit the evidenced workflows.

Record whether PDR-007 should be rejected, superseded, or retained for a different context. Do not erase disconfirming evidence.

## Pilot Evidence Plan

A controlled pilot, if separately approved after implementation, should define baselines, attribution, sources, observation period, exclusions, and data quality before results are interpreted. Capture evidence for:

- opportunities entering the workflow by actionability state;
- ownership established, changed, or left unowned;
- follow-ups due, overdue, and completed using dealer-validated definitions;
- disposition distribution, corrections, reopening, and unresolved items;
- appointments linked through an approved attribution method;
- jobs converted through an approved attribution method;
- recovered revenue and currency only where causal and financial definitions are approved;
- gross profit only where cost data is sufficiently complete and governed;
- aging and handling time;
- suppressed/review-required safety events and inappropriate-action incidents;
- user adoption, abandonment, workaround use, and qualitative usability evidence;
- data completeness, freshness, duplicate rate, and correction burden.

Do not set KPI targets in this package. Pilot results must separate potential revenue, observed workflow outcomes, attributed conversion, recovered revenue, and gross profit. A pilot is not proof of general market performance without appropriate design and replication.
